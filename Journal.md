// 1 - Basic Chassis Build

I started by sketching the long, narrow base of the part, which forms the central spine of the F1 chassis. I extruded this shape and added the wide blocks at the rear to hold the motor and provide attachment points for the rear axle assembly.

// 2 - Adding Mounting Holes
I measured the motor and the axle rod to make sure the part would fit everything I already had. I created the necessary circular cutouts (holes) on the rear blocks for the motor to screw in and for the axle rod to pass through. These holes are crucial for the car to function.


 <img width="1500" height="1316" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8cf1f8b1-b342-4bfe-ad6b-0526e0ef711e" />


// 3 - Cockpit and Nose Shaping

I focused on the front end to make it look like an F1 car. I extruded a small cylindrical/cone shape onto the chassis spine to represent the driver's cockpit/nose section. I blended the front edge with the main body to give it a smooth, aerodynamic look, fitting the F1 style.

// 4 - Front Wing/Spoiler Integration

To complete the F1 look and to make the final model look ready, I added the front wing mounts at the very front of the chassis piece. I also added the rear spoiler/wing element on the back blocks. I checked the piece one last time to make sure all holes were centered and ready for assembly.
